# Everyday Symptoms: General Self-Care

## Common cold
A common cold usually causes a runny or blocked nose, sneezing, sore throat and mild cough. It is caused by viruses, so antibiotics do not help. Most colds improve within 7 to 10 days. Self-care: rest, drink plenty of warm fluids, gargle with warm salt water for a sore throat, and use steam inhalation for a blocked nose. See a doctor if symptoms last more than 10 days, get worse after improving, or breathing becomes difficult.

## Fever
A normal body temperature is about 37 degrees Celsius (98.6 degrees Fahrenheit). A fever is generally a temperature of 38 degrees Celsius (100.4 degrees Fahrenheit) or higher. Self-care: rest, drink plenty of fluids, wear light clothing and keep the room comfortable. See a doctor if the fever lasts more than 3 days, is very high (above 39.4 degrees Celsius or 103 degrees Fahrenheit), or comes with a severe headache, stiff neck, skin rash, confusion or trouble breathing. Fever in a baby under 3 months needs medical attention right away.

## Headache
Most headaches are tension headaches linked to stress, poor sleep, skipped meals, dehydration or long screen time. Self-care: drink water, rest in a quiet dark room, eat regular meals, take short screen breaks and gently stretch the neck and shoulders. See a doctor if headaches are frequent, keep getting worse, or follow a head injury. Get urgent help for a sudden, very severe headache, or a headache with fever and stiff neck, confusion, weakness or vision changes.

## Sore throat
Most sore throats are caused by viruses and improve in a few days. Self-care: warm fluids, gargling with warm salt water, rest and avoiding smoking. See a doctor if the sore throat lasts more than a week, makes swallowing or breathing difficult, or comes with a high fever.

## Diarrhoea and dehydration
Diarrhoea means loose watery stools. The main danger is losing too much body fluid. Self-care: sip fluids often, use oral rehydration solution (ORS), and eat light foods such as rice, banana and curd as tolerated. Signs of dehydration include dark urine, dry mouth, dizziness, extreme thirst and very little urine. See a doctor if diarrhoea lasts more than 2 days, there is blood in the stool, there is a high fever, or you cannot keep fluids down. Children, older adults and pregnant women should get medical advice early.

## Medicines
This assistant cannot recommend medicine names or doses. Always ask a doctor or pharmacist before taking any medicine, especially for children, pregnant women, older adults or people with other health conditions. Do not give aspirin to children. Do not take leftover antibiotics or share prescription medicines.
